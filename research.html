Research

Research Statement

After Use is a practice-based inquiry into what happens to the organising relations once enacted through obsolete paper-based systems after those systems cease to operate. It asks how these relations can be traced and translated into contemporary perceptual and operational conditions without presenting artistic interpretation as historical fact.

Current Research Questions

  • What happens to organising relations after the paper systems that enacted them cease to operate?
  • How can incomplete material remains and historical records be traced without assuming continuity?
  • How can material, spatial, and embodied processes translate former relations into contemporary encounters?
  • Under what conditions can selected relations be tested through operational digital or algorithmically mediated environments?

Provisional Material Scope

  • Commodity labels and small wrappers: recognition, attraction, authenticity, and value
  • Ration coupons and exchange or supply vouchers: eligibility, quantity, location, time, allocation, and exchange
  • A bounded working corpus rather than a complete or representative archive

Methods

  • Corpus construction and evidential protocol
  • Contextual tracing and diagnostic reading
  • Material, spatial, and embodied translation
  • Experimental testing of algorithmic mediation
  • Documentation, comparative analysis, and ethics

Experimental Focus

The computational strand is not primarily concerned with whether a machine reads an object more accurately than a human. Algorithmic mediation is retained only where it changes how a selected relation operates—through decision-making, participation, access, feedback, or possible emergence. If it produces no meaningful distinction, it will be reduced, revised, or removed.

Working Research Sequence

Withdrawal → Residue → Tracing → Translation → Experimental Re-operation → Possible Emergence
