CURRENT PRACTICE / 03

THE BUREAU OF INVALIDATED TICKETS

Research prototype · Working archive · 2026–

Tickets, vouchers, wrappers, labels and other obsolete everyday paper objects.

I am not collecting rare objects. I am tracing everyday systems through the paper they left behind.

Project

What the bureau studies

The project currently follows different ways in which everyday paper entered ordinary life: through allocation, exchange, identification, attraction, memory and use.

The Bureau of Invalidated Tickets gathers obsolete printed matter that once circulated through ordinary life. Some objects functioned as permissions, quotas, payments or records. Others worked through colour, image, attraction and desire. After their original use disappears, these papers remain as material traces of systems, habits, images and everyday life.

The bureau is therefore not a fixed collection or a nostalgic display. It is a provisional research structure: objects are collected, photographed, grouped, filmed and written through in order to test how material traces might reconnect with forms of everyday life that have disappeared.

Research question / RQ–01

What remains in an object after the system it belonged to has disappeared?

01

MOVING-IMAGE STUDY

Process evidence
Duration 02:00

The film follows the bureau as a quiet working process rather than as a finished artwork. Materials are touched, turned, compared and rearranged. The movement of the hand becomes part of the research method.

  • Two-minute research cut with captions and no live recording sound.
  • Focus: handling, grouping, comparison and material attention.
  • Tone: calm documentary study for research presentation.

02

DIFFERENT WAYS OF READING

Tickets / Packaging
Everyday paper

Different materials resist a single mode of reading. Tickets often become more legible through grouping, repetition and regional comparison, while wrappers and labels can remain visually active as individual objects. These differences are not treated as fixed categories, but as observations emerging through handling the archive.

03

CLASSIFICATION AS METHOD

Working method
Provisional taxonomy

Classification is used here as a temporary research method, not as a final taxonomy. Grouping allows differences in region, repetition, value and traces of use to emerge. Some objects become legible through comparison; others require close attention to image, surface or touch. Each material asks to be read differently.

Classification is not the conclusion.
It is a temporary way of seeing.

04

OBSERVATION PAGES

Close reading
Archive notes 01–03

The observation pages slow the project down. Each page follows either a single object or a material group, depending on how the material asks to be read. The page becomes a place to notice what an object regulated, promised or carried, and what remains after its original function disappears.

05

CURRENT INQUIRY

Open questions
Next research cycle

The bureau remains open. The current question is not how to finish the archive, but how obsolete paper can reconnect material traces with forms of everyday life that have disappeared.

  1. 01What remains in an everyday object after its original function disappears?
  2. 02Why do different materials ask to be read at different scales — individually, in groups, or through touch?
  3. 03How can material traces reconnect us with everyday lives we did not experience ourselves?
