CURRENT PRACTICE / 01

AFTER USE

COLLECTED COMMERCIAL PAPER / ARTWORKS AND ONGOING INQUIRY

Umbrella practice · Working collections · 2026–

A broader practice developing contemporary works from collected packaging, wrappers, labels, tickets and related printed matter through comparison, grouping and remaking.

I do not treat collected paper as historical proof. I use it to develop material encounters in which evidence, association and uncertainty can remain visible.

CENTRAL QUESTION / MATERIAL AND HISTORY

How can a work retain material qualities and verifiable everyday commercial connections while allowing incomplete histories to remain incomplete?

Project

Scope and current direction

After Use is the wider artistic exploration. It begins with commercial paper encountered through second-hand markets and personal collecting, without assuming that the materials belong to one period, one system or one recoverable history.

Within that wider practice, the current research proposal focuses more narrowly on commercial packaging, wrappers and paper labels. The Bureau of Invalidated Tickets is a distinct archive and interface subproject retained as an earlier experiment, rather than a name for the whole practice.

Core question / RQ

How can material qualities, commercial connections and historical uncertainty be held together in contemporary artistic forms?

01

2026 ARCHIVE PILOT

Earlier broader field
Retained as a research record

The Bureau of Invalidated Tickets began as a distinct 2026 archive of tickets, vouchers and other printed matter found through street bookstalls, online markets and collector networks. It tested whether scanning and machine-assisted comparison could support material observation.

This material remains available as a working record, not as a recovered historical taxonomy and not as the whole of After Use. The current research proposal works with a narrower set of commercial packaging, wrappers and paper labels, where source research and physical remaking guide the next stage.

02

ARTIST-ORGANISED ARCHIVE

Before machine reading
Situated classification

The materials were already being sorted, compared and interpreted before they became digital records. From the beginning, sugar wrappers and tickets were kept as two distinct material systems in different physical and notational groupings. Tickets were read through exchange, qualification and institutional identity; grain coupons through region; wrappers through colour, attraction, accumulation and desire. These are situated artistic classifications rather than a recovered historical taxonomy.

Artist grouping / Regulated access and exchange

Eligibility, quantity and allocation

Ration coupons and exchange vouchers organised who could obtain what, how much, where and when. Group reading makes their explicit conditions, regional differences, repetition and traces of handling available for comparison.

Eligibility · Quantity · Location · Time · Allocation · Exchange

Artist grouping / Commodity image and value

Recognition, attraction and value

Wrappers and labels participated in naming, recognition, attraction, perceived authenticity and commodity value. Single-object reading keeps colour, typography, printed image and handling trace available without reducing them to visual similarity alone.

Naming · Recognition · Attraction · Authenticity · Commodity value

Peripheral material / Awaiting entry

Held at the edge of the corpus

Matchbox prints, cigarette packaging, stamps and other printed matter remain visible in the working archive without becoming a third equal category. They enter the core corpus only when provenance and analysis show that they extend the research question.

Retained · Unresolved · Awaiting provenance · Conditional entry

How each record is read / 01

What can be seen is kept separate from what is known, interpreted or proposed.

The deeper research terms remain visible inside each record: direct observation, documented context, researcher inference, artistic proposition, machine output and what remains unknown.

FROM PHYSICAL OBJECTS TO DIGITAL RECORDS

The first digital translation does not coincide with the physical archive.

An artist-led inventory identified 318 distinct physical objects after initial system-level separation, then checked them for duplication. The present web archive contains 296 candidate digital crop records produced through a mixed computational and manually adjusted extraction workflow. A digital record may merge several objects, omit an object boundary or preserve a scan condition rather than one independently verified object.

03

WHEN ONE SYSTEM READS ANOTHER

Machine error
Diagnostic evidence

An obsolete administrative system is being read again by a contemporary computational system.

Machine classifications, omissions and misreadings are retained as diagnostic evidence rather than treated as corrected catalogue truth. They reveal which visual and textual properties become computable, which material relations disappear and where a new record is reconstructed by the reading process itself.

T–024 / Segmentation + ontology flattening

One sleeve, fourteen one-fen banknotes.

The artist confirmed that this physical sleeve contains 14 one-fen banknotes marked 1953. The notes were already held together in the same bag; the digital crop represented the stacked set as one candidate record.

The record preserves the appearance of a layered group but collapses fourteen physical items into one digital unit. This segmentation decision precedes later OCR or grouping.

T–083 / High-confidence error

A plausible character breaks the unit.

Relatively high OCR confidence does not prevent 市斤 from becoming 市厅.

T–120 / Evidential absence

The label is read; the blank is lost.

OCR detects 月 but not the meaningful condition that no month was entered.
